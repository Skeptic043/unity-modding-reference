# Mesh UVs and material dependencies in reuse

A familiar material name or a successful spawn does not establish that reused geometry will look like the native reference. The result also depends on mesh UVs, material roles, shader dependencies, sampled-object lifetime and the rendered comparison.

[Rendering](README.md)

## Source visual

The source visual is identified by the exact game, engine, asset and inspection-tool versions plus its `GameObject`, `MeshFilter`, mesh, renderer and material references. Texture references, UVs and the visible regions in an asset preview or controlled render complete that correspondence.

Compare a proposed mesh face by face. A primitive that maps across an entire atlas does not establish the intended finish. Report extracted coordinate summaries and complete inspection commands without redistributing proprietary meshes or textures.

## Material roles and dependencies

Each material role can carry different shader properties, render queues, keywords, authored colors and surface parameters. Renderer dependencies can also remain on the prefab or inactive customization objects rather than only in exported material entries.

Exercise the production traversal with missing exported materials but retained renderer dependencies. A serialized material match or successful construction log does not establish visible compatibility, distance effects or transitions.

## Runtime evidence

If runtime reuse is appropriate, use verified UVs, a clear missing-asset fallback and ownership that does not depend on the sampled scene object remaining alive. Otherwise use an owned visual approximation and document that choice.

Compare the result beside the native object in daylight and at night. Source and asset inspection do not establish lighting, color match, collider fit or the final appearance in a player. Recheck after changes to the mesh, atlas, shader, dependency traversal or rendering environment.
